Install mirror cabinet in downstairs bathroom

Posted on 21 August 2026

The downstairs bathroom has no mirror and no cabinet. The only surfaces in the room that hold anything are the basin rim and the tiled window reveal, which is in daily use for toiletries.

The cabinet has been bought and is already on site: an Emielia Battery Powered Illuminated LED Mirror Cabinet, 600 mm wide, 600 mm high and 135 mm deep, weighing 11.12 kg empty. It arrives assembled and needs wall-mounting.

What the unit is:

  • Battery powered, running on eight AA cells, so no mains supply, spur or electrician is involved. The cabinet ships without cells; eight are on site so the lights can be tested as soon as it is up.
  • No demister pad and no shaver socket. The mains version of the same cabinet, at the same size, has both. This is not that one.
  • Two mirrored doors opening to 110 degrees, each with a vertical LED strip, and a rocker switch on the right-hand side.
  • Two fixed shelves, not adjustable, in a polished stainless steel carcase.
  • Rated IP44, for bathroom Zone 2.

1 / 3

It goes centred above the basin. The basin is 430 mm wide with its rim 820 mm off the floor, so the cabinet will overhang it by roughly 85 mm on each side.

The wall there is tiled in the same marble-effect finish as the rest of the room, and what lies behind the tile is not known. That is the first thing to establish, because it decides the fixings.

Four things the fixing has to account for:

  • The substrate is unidentified. Whether the fixings land in masonry, in studs, or in a cavity needing rated anchors is currently an open question.
  • The fixing centres are not published. There are four mounting holes in bars on the inside back panel, and the centres have to be taken off the unit itself.
  • The basin's supplies and waste run below, in or against the same wall. The fixing area needs scanning for concealed services before anything is drilled.
  • Four mounting screws come in the cabinet's hardware pack. Wall plugs and anchors do not, and cannot be chosen until the wall is identified.

The manufacturer's recommended height is 1626 mm from the finished floor to the centre of the mirror, which would leave the bottom edge about 500 mm above the basin rim. Settle the final height on site with the cabinet held in place before marking.

Materials

  • Wall plugs or cavity anchors rated for the loaded cabinet, once the wall construction is known
  • Eight AA cells, already on site
  • Sanitary silicone
  • Packing shims
DIY fallback Limited

Most of this is within reach, and the usual reason a mirror cabinet is not is absent here: the unit runs on batteries, so there is no supply to bring in and nothing to isolate. The part that is not DIY is choosing fixings blind. An 11.12 kg cabinet plus its contents on the wrong anchors comes off the wall, so the substrate has to be identified rather than guessed.

Fallback approach

  • Establish what is behind the tile before anything else. Check for a stud pattern and scan the whole fixing area for pipes and cables.
  • Take the four mounting-hole centres off the back of the cabinet itself, then mark them out on a level line with the cabinet still safely on the floor.
  • Land the fixings on studs or solid masonry where possible. If a mark falls in a cavity with nothing behind it, use anchors rated for the loaded weight rather than shifting the cabinet to suit.
  • Drill the tile with a ceramic or diamond bit at low speed and hammer action switched off, getting through the glaze before increasing pressure, then change to whatever the substrate behind needs.
  • Mount it with a second physically capable adult supporting the cabinet throughout, shim behind the carcase if the tiled face is not flat, and seal the penetrations.
  • Fit eight AA cells in the battery compartment inside the cabinet, then check both LED strips and the door alignment before finishing.

Tools and supplies

  • Wall detector
  • Tape measure and spirit level or cross-line laser
  • Ceramic or diamond tile bits, plus masonry or wood bits for the substrate
  • Drill-driver whose hammer action can be switched off
  • Wall plugs or anchors rated for the loaded cabinet
  • Sanitary silicone, packing shims, eye protection and a second adult

Stop and hand over if

  • What is behind the tile cannot be established with confidence.
  • The scan suggests a pipe or cable anywhere in the fixing area.
  • A tile begins to craze or chip, or a fixing will not pull up tight.
  • The cabinet cannot be supported continuously by a second capable person.
