Install toilet roll holder in downstairs bathroom

Posted on 21 August 2026

The downstairs bathroom has no toilet roll holder. The WC stands on the rear wall with bare tile either side of it, so there is nowhere to keep a roll and nowhere to set a phone down.

The holder has been bought and is already on site: a brushed-gold roll bar with an integral shelf on top, 135 mm wide, 85 mm tall and 75 mm deep, weighing 0.39 kg. Loaded with a roll and a phone it is still comfortably under 1 kg.

It goes up on its 3M adhesive pads, not on screws. The tile is new, what sits behind it is not known, and at this weight the pads carry it — so the wall stays unpierced. The screw fixings are in the box as a fallback if a pad ever lets go.

1 / 2

What the adhesive needs in order to hold:

  • Bare, dry, degreased tile. Cleaner or polish residue is the usual reason a pad fails. Wipe the area with isopropyl alcohol and let it flash off.
  • An unbroken run of tile. A grout line under the pad leaves it touching only at the edges.
  • One shot at the position. The pad grabs on contact and cannot be slid true afterwards, so mark the line before peeling the backing.
  • 24 hours before it carries anything. Press it home firmly and keep the roll and the phone off it until then.

It goes in the gap between the WC and the bath, which is taped at 0.30 m. The holder is 135 mm wide, so centred it leaves roughly 80 mm clear on each side.

Set the shelf top level with the cistern, at 0.82 m. That puts the roll bar near 0.74 m, which is 0.27 m above the closed lid and within easy reach from seated, and it keeps a single line running across the wall.

Materials

  • Isopropyl alcohol and a lint-free cloth
  • Masking tape for marking
DIY fallback Reasonable

This is a job to do rather than to book. The holder weighs 0.39 kg, it mounts on adhesive pads, and nothing is drilled, cut or connected. The two ways it goes wrong are a contaminated tile face and loading it before the adhesive has taken.

Fallback approach

  • Hold the holder in the gap between the WC and the bath and check it centres with roughly 80 mm clear either side.
  • Mark the shelf line at 0.82 m, level with the cistern top, using a spirit level and a strip of masking tape rather than a pencil mark on the tile.
  • Wipe the tile with isopropyl alcohol and let it dry fully. Bathroom spray cleaner is not a substitute — its residue is what pads fail on.
  • Peel the backing, place it in one movement, and press hard for 30 seconds.
  • Leave it 24 hours before putting a roll or a phone on it.

Tools and supplies

  • Tape measure and spirit level
  • Masking tape
  • Isopropyl alcohol and a lint-free cloth

Stop and hand over if

  • The tile is chipped, crazed or lifting where the pad would land.
  • The pad cannot sit on an unbroken run of tile without crossing a grout line.
  • The adhesive will not hold and the holder has to be screwed instead, because what lies behind the tile is unknown.
