Stop the leak at the island sink trap

Posted on 21 August 2026

The kitchen island sink leaks into the cupboard below whenever it drains. Nothing escapes while the sink is standing idle. A plastic container sits under the trap catching the drips, and is emptied by hand.

The water comes from the union joint on the white plastic trap, at the left-hand of the two large threaded nuts on top of the U-bend. A dried brown trail runs from under that nut, down the outside of the bend, to its lowest point. That is where it drips, and where the container has been put.

Because it appears only while the sink drains, the joint carries no pressure. Three things to rule out, in this order:

  1. The nut. A plastic trap union seals hand-tight. One that has worked loose, or was never seated fully, weeps only when water passes through it.
  2. The washer. Once the nut is known to be tight, a washer that is displaced, distorted, perished or missing is the usual cause.
  3. The trap body and thread. A hairline crack at the shoulder or a stripped thread means the trap is replaced rather than resealed.

Anything fitted has to keep what is already there. The trap discharges into the grey waste pipe that drops through the cupboard base and leaves the island through a floor penetration, and corrugated appliance hose runs behind the same pipework. Establish what ties into this trap before taking it apart.

Nothing under the sink is damaged. The container has caught everything so far, and the cupboard base beneath it is dry. That holds only for as long as the box is emptied, and the base is a faced board that will swell once it is properly wet.

Materials

  • Replacement washers for the trap unions
  • A like-for-like replacement trap, if the body or thread is damaged
DIY fallback Reasonable

The joint is on the waste side, so it holds no pressure and nothing needs isolating. Re-seating the washer on a plastic trap union is a fair first attempt. It stops being one as soon as the trap body or its thread turns out to be damaged.

Fallback approach

  • Clear the cupboard and put a bowl and cloths under the trap. The U-bend holds standing water and it will come out.
  • Try the nut first. Plastic trap unions do up hand-tight only, and grips will crack the nut or the trap shoulder. Nip it up by hand, run the tap, and watch the joint.
  • If it still weeps, unscrew that nut by hand and slide it clear. Check the washer for splits, distortion and grit, and whether it was sitting square.
  • Wipe the pipe end and the washer seat clean, re-seat the washer the right way round, and do the nut back up hand-tight.
  • Dry the whole bend, then run the sink and check the joint with dry tissue rather than by eye, because a slow weep is easier to feel than to see.

Tools and supplies

  • A bowl to catch the water held in the trap
  • Cloths and dry paper towel
  • Torch

Stop and hand over if

  • The nut will not nip up by hand, or turns without ever gripping.
  • There is a crack, split or distortion anywhere in the trap body or the nut.
  • The leak continues after the washer has been re-seated square and sound.
  • Water appears anywhere other than the trap, particularly at the copper supply pipes at the back of the cupboard.
