Install privacy locks and security bolts on both bedroom doors

Posted on 23 August 2026

Neither bedroom door can currently be secured from inside. Each door needs two separate forms of locking: an everyday privacy lock and a heavy-duty internal bolt intended to give occupants additional protection if they need to shelter in the bedroom during an intrusion.

The privacy locks remain fully in scope. Fit a compatible bathroom-style turn-and-release lock to each door, providing:

  • An internal thumbturn that is easy to operate from inside the bedroom.
  • An external emergency release, operable with a coin or flat screwdriver rather than a key.
  • A bathroom mortice lock or integrated privacy latch compatible with the existing handles, door thickness and backset.
  • Visible furniture that matches the existing finish as closely as practical, with any redundant holes or finish damage made good.

Also fit a separate security bolt high on the inside face of each door:

  • Use one heavy-duty surface-mounted tower bolt per door, positioned at the top and operable without a key from inside the bedroom.
  • Select robust bolts and matching keeps intended for security use, rather than light-duty cupboard or bathroom bolts.
  • Fix each keep into solid door-frame structure, not surface trim alone, and use fixings suited to the door and frame construction.
  • Make the bolt readily reachable by the intended occupants and ensure it slides fully into its keep without binding.
  • If either door or frame is too light to provide a credible fixing, identify the reinforcement or alternative security hardware required before installation.

Confirm the construction of both doors and frames before ordering or cutting. If either is a fire door, all selected hardware and fitting methods must preserve its fire performance and the required escape arrangements. Test the privacy thumbturn, outside emergency release and tower bolt with each door open and closed before the job is signed off.

Materials

  • Two compatible bathroom mortice locks or integrated privacy latches
  • Matching turn-and-release furniture, if separate from the chosen lock cases
  • Two heavy-duty surface-mounted tower bolts, one for each door, with robust matching keeps
  • Security-suitable fixings matched to the door and frame construction
  • Door or frame reinforcement, only if inspection shows it is needed and an appropriate method is agreed
  • Wood filler and matching touch-up finish, if required
DIY fallback Limited

The privacy hardware may be achievable DIY, but the tower bolts are intended to resist forced entry. Fit them only where the door and frame provide verified solid fixing points; a neat surface fixing into weak material would not meet the security purpose.

Fallback approach

  • Inspect and measure both doors before buying anything, including the door thickness, construction, backset, spindle position and available depth for the privacy-lock case.
  • Confirm whether either door is fire-rated and locate solid structure for both the bolt body and its keep.
  • Select robust surface-mounted tower bolts whose keeps can be fixed securely to the available frame structure, and confirm that the proposed top positions remain easy to reach from inside.
  • Use the supplied template to mark the lock case, spindle and thumb-turn positions, then drill and chisel in small stages.
  • Align each tower bolt for full, smooth engagement, drill correctly sized pilot holes and fasten both the body and keep into solid material with suitable fixings.
  • Test the privacy lock, external emergency release and tower bolt repeatedly with each door open and closed before making good any redundant holes.

Tools and supplies

  • Tape measure and combination square
  • Drill-driver with suitable wood bits or hole saw
  • Sharp wood chisels and mallet
  • Screwdrivers
  • Lock-fitting jig, if required by the chosen case
  • Stable step platform for the high-level bolt positions
  • Eye protection and dust extraction

Stop and hand over if

  • The door or frame construction is unclear, hollow or too weak to provide solid fixings for the security bolt and keep.
  • Either door is fire-rated and the proposed hardware or alteration is not approved for it.
  • The top position cannot be reached and released quickly by the intended occupants.
  • The bolt cannot engage fully without binding, or its keep would rely on surface trim rather than structural timber.
  • The latch, thumbturn, emergency release or tower bolt does not operate freely with the door both open and closed.
