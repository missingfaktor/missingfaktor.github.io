Upgrade garden side-gate lock

Posted on 22 July 2026

The existing latch and sliding bolt on the timber side gate are awkward to use and do not provide a convenient keyed lock from both sides. Relocate the existing Norfolk-style latch to a more practical position, then supply and fit a weatherproof, key-operated long-throw gate lock with a securely fixed keep.

1 / 3

Materials

  • Weatherproof long-throw gate lock with keys
  • Matching keep
  • Exterior-grade through-bolts or security fixings
DIY fallback Reasonable

This is plausible DIY joinery if the timber gate and post are sound, the chosen long-throw lock fits the available geometry, and the gate can be held square while the lock and keep are aligned.

Fallback approach

  • Check the gate for sag, rot and loose hinges, then clamp or support it in its normal closed position.
  • Lay out the relocated latch, lock body and keep with a square, confirming access from both sides and full bolt engagement.
  • Drill and chisel in stages, protect every new cut face, and prefer through-bolts or security fixings where the hardware permits.
  • Test the key, latch and bolt from both sides with the gate open and closed before final tightening.

Tools and supplies

  • Drill-driver with long wood bits and the required hole saw
  • Wood chisels and rubber mallet
  • Tape measure and combination square
  • Clamps and temporary gate support
  • Socket set and screwdrivers
  • Exterior-grade fixings, eye protection and hearing protection

Stop and hand over if

  • The gate or post is rotten, split, badly out of square or unable to hold the fixings.
  • The lock needs major structural cutting or cannot align with a securely mounted keep.
  • The gate must be removed or lifted without adequate support and another capable person.
