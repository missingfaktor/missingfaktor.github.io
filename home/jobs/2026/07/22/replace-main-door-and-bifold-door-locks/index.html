Replace main-door and bifold-door locks

Posted on 22 July 2026

Both external doors have new cylinders, so keys issued before the change no longer open either door. The main entrance, which was stiff to turn in both directions, now locks and unlocks smoothly and its multipoint mechanism was left sound.

1 / 2

What was fitted, as described by the locksmith:

  • Main entrance: a 1-star cylinder.
  • Bifold door: an anti-snap cylinder, with no rating quoted.
  • The two doors are not keyed alike. Each takes its own key.
  • The make and model of both remain unrecorded.

Neither is a restricted key system, so there is no security card to hold and either key can be copied at any key-cutting shop. Key control therefore extends only to the keys issued before the change, which no longer work.

The cylinder removed from the main entrance carried a Sold Secure Diamond marking, the highest anti-snap grade. 1-star is the entry level of TS007 and is rated as secure only when paired with a 2-star security handle. Whether the door is as strong as it was therefore depends on the handle now fitted to it, which is stamped with its own kitemark and star rating.

DIY fallback Reasonable

Nothing physical remains. What is left is reading the rating off the main-door handle and deciding what it means, which needs no tools and no work on the doors.

Fallback approach

  • Look for a kitemark and star rating on the main-door handle, usually stamped on the backplate or the handle edge, and photograph it.
  • Ask the locksmith for the make and model of both cylinders fitted.
  • If the handle is not 2-star, price a 3-star cylinder against a 2-star handle before deciding whether to change anything.
  • Record how many keys exist for each door, since any of them can be copied freely.

Tools and supplies

  • Phone camera

Stop and hand over if

  • Reading the rating would mean removing the handle or the cylinder.
  • Either door starts binding, sticking or lifting again to lock.
  • The decision comes out in favour of different hardware, which is a return visit rather than a DIY change.
