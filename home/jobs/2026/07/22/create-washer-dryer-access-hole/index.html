Create washer-dryer access hole

Posted on 22 July 2026

The appliance in place is an LG FWY916WBTN1 washer-dryer. Its water, waste and power connections all sit in the upper part of the back panel, and the existing opening in the cabinet side panel is too low for them. Routing the hoses down to that opening and back up to the waste would leave a dip in the drain hose where water stands. A second opening is needed higher up the same panel.

1 / 2

What the machine itself dictates:

  • 600 mm wide, 850 mm high and 565 mm deep, and 1100 mm deep with the door open.
  • Cold fill only. A single inlet that must not be connected to hot water.
  • Condensing drying, so nothing needs venting and the condensate leaves through the drain hose.
  • At least 20 mm clearance at the sides and 100 mm behind, so the hoses run in that rear gap before they reach the panel.

Three things have to pass through the opening:

  • The cold fill hose, about 40 mm across at its machine-end elbow.
  • The drain hose, about 30 mm on its corrugated body, with a wider moulded end.
  • The moulded 13 A plug, roughly 50 by 45 mm, if the socket is inside the cabinet.

The plug, not the hoses, sets the size. 68 mm takes all three and is a standard hole-saw size, and the workable range is 60 to 80 mm. Below 60 mm the plug will not pass; above 80 mm the panel is needlessly weakened and the edge is harder to trim neatly. If the socket turns out to be above the worktop and only the hoses pass, 50 mm is the floor and 57 mm is comfortable.

The centre wants to be 700 to 800 mm above the finished floor, with 750 to 780 mm the best target. Three limits set that band:

  • LG requires that no part of the drain hose sits above 1000 mm, which on its own caps the hole near 900 mm.
  • The cabinet caps it lower. A standard base-unit side panel stops about 870 mm above the floor, and a 68 mm hole needs its top edge around 40 mm clear of that, putting the highest usable centre near 795 mm.
  • Below about 700 mm the run starts to fall away from the machine's connections and then climb again to reach the waste, which is the dip worth avoiding.

Set the hole back towards the rear of the panel, its centre roughly 460 to 530 mm behind the cabinet's front edge, so the hoses run straight back instead of diagonally across the machine. Leave at least 40 mm of solid panel between the hole and any edge, shelf, hinge plate or carcass fixing, and at least 50 mm between it and the existing low opening.

These measurements fix the final position:

  • The heights of the cold inlet, the drain outlet and the cable exit on the back of the machine as it stands.
  • The height of the panel's top edge and of the shelves inside the cabinet.
  • The height and type of the waste connection and the isolation valve inside the cabinet.
  • Whether the socket is inside the cabinet or above the worktop.

Materials

  • 68 mm hole saw and arbor
  • Split grommet or edge trim sized to the finished hole
  • Clear sealant or edge lacquer for the cut edge
DIY fallback Reasonable

This is a manageable DIY job. The diameter and height band are settled; what remains is confirming the connection heights on the machine and finding a clear line through the panel that misses the shelves and fixings.

Fallback approach

  • Measure the heights of the cold inlet, drain outlet and cable exit on the back of the machine, and check whether the plug has to pass through the panel at all.
  • Mark a centre 700 to 800 mm above the floor and 460 to 530 mm behind the cabinet front, then check both faces of the panel for shelves, hinge plates, carcass fixings, pipes and wiring before committing to it.
  • Clamp a sacrificial backing board behind the panel to reduce breakout, then cut with a 68 mm hole saw, easing off as the pilot breaks through.
  • Vacuum the dust, sand the cut edge smooth, seal or trim it, then route the hoses so the drain hose has no dip and no part of it sits above 1000 mm.

Tools and supplies

  • Drill-driver
  • 68 mm hole saw and arbor
  • Tape measure and spirit level
  • Wall or service detector
  • Clamps and sacrificial backing board
  • Sandpaper and edge trim or grommet
  • Vacuum, eye protection and hearing protection

Stop and hand over if

  • The only workable centre would clip a shelf, hinge plate or carcass fixing.
  • A pipe, cable or structural cabinet member may be behind the mark.
  • No centre in the 700 to 800 mm band leaves 40 mm of sound panel all round.
  • The waste connection cannot take the drain hose without a dip or a rise above 1000 mm.
