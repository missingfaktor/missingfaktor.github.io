Create washer-dryer access hole

Posted on 22 July 2026

The appliance in place is an LG FWY916WBTN1 washer-dryer, and its cold inlet, drain hose and power cable all leave the upper part of the back panel. The existing opening through the divider sits too low for them. Taking the services down to it and back up to the plumbing risks kinks, a trapped low section and strain on the connections, so a second opening is needed higher up the same panel.

The opening goes through the divider between the washer-dryer bay and the sink cupboard beside it, which is the panel the two share. The cupboard door hinges onto the front edge of that same panel, so its hinge plates are an obstruction near the front.

1 / 2

The divider has been measured from inside the cupboard. These are the cupboard's own build-up, not heights above the finished floor:

  • 154 mm top fascia or front rail, directly under the worktop.
  • 380 mm clear upper compartment.
  • 18 mm middle shelf.
  • 300 mm clear lower compartment.
  • 18 mm base panel, making 870 mm in all.

The hole belongs in the 380 mm upper compartment, above the middle shelf. The 154 mm band above that is a fascia or front rail, so the divider may well be clear behind it. If it is, a centre higher than the compartment's nominal top is possible, and worth having, because it sits closer to the machine's own connections.

What the machine itself dictates:

  • 600 mm wide, 850 mm high and 565 mm deep.
  • A single cold inlet, so only one fill hose passes through the panel.
  • Condensing drying, so there is no vent duct to accommodate and the condensate leaves through the drain hose.
  • At least 20 mm clearance at the sides and 100 mm behind, so the hoses turn in that rear gap before they reach the panel.
  • No part of the drain hose above 1000 mm, which governs the run inside the cupboard as much as the hole.

Three things have to pass through the opening:

  • The cold fill hose, about 40 mm across at its machine-end elbow.
  • The drain hose, about 30 mm on its corrugated body, with a wider moulded end.
  • The moulded 13 A plug, roughly 50 by 45 mm, if the socket is inside the cupboard.

The plug, not the hoses, sets the size. 68 mm takes all three and is a standard hole-saw size, and the workable range is 60 to 80 mm. Below 60 mm the plug will not pass; above 80 mm the panel is needlessly weakened and the edge is harder to trim neatly. If the socket turns out to be above the worktop and only the hoses pass, 50 mm is the floor and 57 mm is comfortable.

Set the centre 60 to 100 mm forward of the divider's rear edge, taken off the panel itself rather than assumed from a standard cabinet depth, so the hoses run straight back instead of diagonally across the machine. Leave at least 40 mm of intact panel between the hole and any shelf, panel edge, hinge plate or carcass fixing, and at least 50 mm between it and the existing low opening.

These measurements fix the final position:

  • The heights of the cold inlet, the drain outlet and the cable exit on the back of the machine, taken above the finished floor.
  • The height of the middle shelf and of the fascia's lower edge above the finished floor.
  • Whether the 154 mm fascia runs the full depth of the cupboard or only across the front.
  • The positions of the waste connection and the isolation valve inside the cupboard, and how far the sink bowl and trap come down into it.
  • Whether the socket is inside the cupboard or above the worktop.

Materials

  • 68 mm hole saw and arbor
  • Split grommet or edge trim sized to the finished hole
  • Clear sealant or edge lacquer for the cut edge
DIY fallback Reasonable

This is a manageable DIY job. The diameter is settled and the divider has been measured; what remains is confirming the connection heights on the machine and finding a line through the upper compartment that misses the shelf, the hinges and the sink plumbing.

Fallback approach

  • Measure the heights of the cold inlet, drain outlet and cable exit on the back of the machine, and check whether the plug has to pass through the panel at all.
  • Mark a centre in the 380 mm upper compartment, 60 to 100 mm forward of the divider's rear edge, then check both faces of the panel for the shelf, hinge plates, carcass fixings, sink plumbing and wiring before committing to it.
  • Clamp a sacrificial backing board behind the panel to reduce breakout, then cut with a 68 mm hole saw, easing off as the pilot breaks through.
  • Vacuum the dust, sand the cut edge smooth, seal or trim it, then route the hoses with no kink, no trapped low section and no part of the drain hose above 1000 mm.

Tools and supplies

  • Drill-driver
  • 68 mm hole saw and arbor
  • Tape measure and spirit level
  • Wall or service detector
  • Clamps and sacrificial backing board
  • Sandpaper and edge trim or grommet
  • Vacuum, eye protection and hearing protection

Stop and hand over if

  • The only workable centre would clip the middle shelf, a hinge plate or a carcass fixing.
  • A pipe, cable or structural cabinet member may be behind the mark.
  • No centre in the upper compartment leaves 40 mm of sound panel all round.
  • The waste connection cannot take the drain hose without a kink or a trapped low section.
