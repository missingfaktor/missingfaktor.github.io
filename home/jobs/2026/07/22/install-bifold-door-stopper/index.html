Install bifold-door magnetic hold-open stops

Posted on 22 July 2026

The opening leaf of the bifold door can move and slam in strong winds, and is currently held back with loose bricks. The previously bought top-mounted item is a uPVC window stay, not hardware designed for the weight of a bifold door, and will be returned rather than fitted. A purpose-made magnetic patio-door stop is the preferred route because it can provide both the buffer and the hold-open.

Shortlisted hardware:

  • Preferred: a pair of Doorstopworld 150mm Patio Doorstops, 90°, With Magnetic Hold, Anthracite Grey, for a Normal door. The supplier describes this size as suitable for bifolds and doors over 850mm wide, and recommends one stop at the top and one at the bottom for a wider door.
  • Angle fallback: the same magnetic 150mm Patio Doorstop in 72°, but only if a cardboard template shows that the 90° version will not meet the open door squarely.
  • Wider-head fallback: a 150mm WideTopStop with magnetic hold and a 75mm top section, in 90° or 72° as templated, only if the standard head cannot land fully on a safe part of the aluminium frame.
  • Standard product: https://doorstopworld.com/shop/150mm-patio-doorstop/
  • WideTopStop alternative: https://doorstopworld.com/shop/widetopstop-doorstop/

Confirm before ordering and drilling:

  1. Make the supplier's 90° and 72° cardboard templates and check both intended positions with the door held fully open. The rubber and magnet must meet the door squarely rather than on an edge.
  2. Confirm solid substrate behind the render for both brackets and a manufacturer-safe fixing zone for each magnet on the aluminium leaf.
  3. Choose the standard head if it makes full contact. Use the 75mm WideTopStop only if the extra width is needed for a safe, square landing area.
  4. Keep both stops aligned so they share the wind load without twisting the door.

Materials

  • Two Doorstopworld 150mm Patio Doorstops with magnetic hold, in Anthracite Grey for a Normal door; 90° preferred, with 72° subject to templating
  • Manufacturer-supplied magnetic plates and stainless-steel fixings
  • Alternative: 150mm WideTopStop with 75mm head if the standard head does not make safe, full contact
  • Additional exterior-grade fixings suited to the substrate behind the render, if the supplied fixings are not appropriate
DIY fallback Limited

The shortlisted pair can be fitted as DIY only after the angle, head size and safe fixing points are confirmed at both the wall and the aluminium leaf. The door is heavy and wind-loaded, so this is not a trial-and-error drilling job.

Fallback approach

  • Make the supplier's 90° and 72° cardboard templates and use them to confirm square contact at the intended top and bottom positions.
  • On a calm day, have a second adult hold the door while both bracket and magnet positions are marked.
  • Confirm the solid substrate behind the render and a manufacturer-safe fixing zone on the aluminium before drilling.
  • Fit both brackets with suitable stainless-steel fixings, following the manufacturer's instructions and protecting their coating.
  • Fit each magnet to the agreed frame position, using a hand screwdriver for the supplied stainless-steel magnet screw, then test the pair gently before exposing the door to wind.

Tools and supplies

  • Tape measure, level and cardboard angle templates
  • Wall detector
  • Combi drill with suitable masonry bit and 2.3mm metal pilot bit
  • Hand screwdriver
  • Exterior-grade fixings
  • A second adult to control the door

Stop and hand over if

  • The substrate behind the render or the construction of the door leaf is uncertain.
  • Either stop cannot meet the door squarely, or the two contact points cannot be aligned without twisting the leaf.
  • The proposed magnet fixing would enter glass, a seal, a drainage channel, a multipoint mechanism or a thermally broken section.
  • The door cannot be held securely and without strain while it is marked and fitted.
