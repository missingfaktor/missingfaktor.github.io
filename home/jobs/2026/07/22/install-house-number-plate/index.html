Install house number plate

Posted on 22 July 2026

No number is displayed at the main entrance, so the house is difficult to identify from the road. The plate has now been supplied and is on site.

It is a solar-powered illuminated plate with a black face and raised gold-effect number and street name.

  • A solar panel is set into the top left of the face, and the LEDs switch on at dusk and off at dawn on their own.
  • It is rated IP66 and needs no mains connection: an internal battery charges from the panel.
  • A Type-C port on the side allows a direct charge at 5V when daylight has been insufficient.
  • An on/off switch sits on the back.

The plate hangs rather than screws through. Two keyhole hangers are moulded into the back near the top corners, so it drops onto two screw heads left standing proud of the wall, and the screw centres have to match the hangers. A shallow recess with a single hole sits at the centre of the back; its purpose is not confirmed. Two light-duty plastic wall plugs and two screws are supplied with the plate.

1 / 2

The proposed position is the painted rendered wall between the front window and the main door frame. What the render is applied to has not been established, and that decides the fixings: the supplied plugs would suit solid masonry behind a thin render, but not a render coat over external insulation board. A hairline crack runs across this section of wall, so the fixing holes should be kept clear of it.

The plate should be switched on and tested before any holes are drilled, by covering the solar panel to see whether the LEDs light.

Materials

  • Customer-supplied solar number plate
  • Two plastic wall plugs and screws supplied with the plate
  • Exterior-grade fixings matched to the wall build-up, if the supplied ones are unsuitable
  • Exterior sealant, if required
DIY fallback Reasonable

This is reasonable DIY work. The plate is self-contained and solar-powered, so no electrical connection is involved, and it hangs on two screws. The one thing to settle first is what the render is fixed to.

Fallback approach

  • Peel the protective film from the face, switch the plate on at the back and cover the solar panel to confirm the LEDs light. Charge it through the Type-C port at 5V if the output is weak.
  • Measure the centres of the two keyhole hangers and mark them out on masking tape, keeping the position clear of the hairline crack.
  • Scan the marked positions for wiring, pipework and metalwork, and check the plate will not foul the door, the window reveal or the door swing.
  • Drill with a bit matched to the wall build-up, clear the dust and fit fixings that suit it rather than defaulting to the supplied plugs.
  • Leave the screw heads proud by the depth of the keyhole slots, hang the plate, check it is level and seal around the holes if water could track in.

Tools and supplies

  • Tape measure, masking tape and spirit level or cross-line laser
  • Wall detector
  • Combi or SDS drill with suitable masonry bit
  • Dust extraction
  • Screwdriver or driver bits
  • Exterior sealant and caulking gun if required
  • Eye protection

Stop and hand over if

  • The detector indicates wiring, pipework or metalwork at the chosen position.
  • The render turns out to sit on insulation board rather than masonry, and no suitable fixing for that build-up is to hand.
  • The render is loose, hollow-sounding or cracking further around the drilled hole.
  • The mounting position requires unsafe ladder work.
