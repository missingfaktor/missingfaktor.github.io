Build fitted wardrobes in two bedrooms

Posted on 29 July 2026

Neither the ground-floor bedroom nor the first-floor second bedroom has any built-in storage. Two fitted wardrobes are to be made and installed against the walls shown in the model views. The schematics below give the carcass sizes, the bay widths, and the internal layout of each bay, and are the reference for all of it.

1 / 2

Every compartment sits behind doors. No niche or shelf is open to the room. Where a schematic reads “open storage”, that means a compartment with no drawer front, still enclosed by the doors in front of it.

Two things the schematics do not show on their face:

  • The compartment heights in a bay add up to the full external carcass height. Carcass, shelf, plinth and top-rail thicknesses are to be absorbed within those figures, not added to them.
  • The three bays of the first-floor wardrobe are not identical inside. The middle bay omits the second shelf compartment so that its hanging space can run higher; the outer two keep both. Every horizontal division below that still lines up across all three bays.

Both wardrobes must be lit inside. This is a firm requirement, not an optional extra, and the wiring route has to be planned before the carcasses are built rather than added afterwards.

  • Warm white LED strip or bar in every bay of both wardrobes, positioned so the light source is not in direct view.
  • Door-activated switching, so a bay lights when its door is opened.
  • Drivers and cabling concealed within the carcass, with no visible trunking.
  • A concealed fused spur rather than a trailing plug lead. If there is no supply near either wall, that part of the work needs an electrician.

Materials:

  • MDF for every door and drawer front. A shaker profile cannot be made in MFC — routing the face cuts through the melamine and exposes the chipboard core, so MFC only makes flat slab fronts.
  • MFC for carcasses, shelves and drawer boxes, with matching ABS edging on visible edges.
  • MDF also for plinths, back panels and scribe fillets.
  • Matt throughout. No gloss, and no woodgrain or patterned decor: plain solid colour.
  • Adjustable shelves on standard shelf pins, at a pitch that allows them to be moved later.

The MDF fronts can be finished two ways. The choice decides whether the colours below are achievable, so it needs settling early:

  • Sprayed lacquer — cut and routed to the shaker profile, primed, and spray-finished in 2-pack polyurethane. Any colour, matched to a RAL or paint-brand reference. Typically 22 mm board. The more expensive route.
  • Vinyl or foil wrap — the profile is pressed and wrapped at the factory. Typically 16–18 mm. Cheaper, but the colour has to be picked from the wrapper's standard range, and the weak point is the edges and seams, where a wrap can lift over time.
  • Wrap peeling is mostly a kitchen failure, caused by heat and steam from ovens and kettles. Neither wardrobe is in a kitchen, so that risk is much lower here — but the edge detail is still where a wrap goes first.

Doors and carcasses are different boards finished by different processes, so they will not match by being ordered to the same colour name. The door and carcass samples must be approved together, side by side, before anything is ordered. Visible outer surfaces — end panels, plinth and top rail — should match the doors rather than the carcass interiors.

One solid colour per wardrobe. The values below are indicative for discussion only. A custom colour like these needs the sprayed route; a wrapped door means taking the nearest colour in the wrap range instead.

  • Eucalyptus green #6e7f6d Ground-floor wardrobe.
  • Garnet red #733635 First-floor wardrobe.

Style is simple Scandinavian: quiet, solid and unfussy.

  • Shaker doors and drawer fronts throughout, on both wardrobes: a plain frame around a recessed flat centre panel. Flat slab fronts and fully flush trimless detailing are not wanted.
  • Slim plain handles, bar or tubular, in one finish across both wardrobes.
  • Hinged doors, two to a bay, with separate shorter doors over the upper compartments as drawn.
  • Soft-close hinges and drawer runners throughout.
  • Scribed to the walls, floor and ceiling. Both rooms should be assumed to be out of true.

On the first-floor wardrobe, both tall doors of the middle bay are to carry full-length mirrors. The mirror takes the place of the centre panel and runs the full height of the door, rather than a small mirror set into a larger front. Safety-backed, and held by the shaker frame rather than fixed on afterwards.

Materials

  • MDF for every shaker door and drawer front, either 22 mm for spraying or 16–18 mm wrapped
  • 2-pack polyurethane spray finish, or factory vinyl wrap, whichever route is chosen
  • MFC panels for the ground-floor carcasses in the nearest matt eucalyptus green decor
  • MFC panels for the first-floor carcasses in the nearest matt garnet red decor
  • Matching ABS edging
  • MDF for plinths, back panels and scribe fillets
  • Hanging rails and end supports
  • Soft-close hinges, drawer runners and shelf pins
  • Safety-backed full-length mirrors for both tall doors of the first-floor middle bay
  • Slim bar handles in a single finish
  • Warm white LED strips, drivers and door switches
