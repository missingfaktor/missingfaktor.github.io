Build fitted wardrobes in two bedrooms

Posted on 29 July 2026

Neither the ground-floor bedroom nor the first-floor second bedroom has any built-in storage. Two fitted wardrobes are to be made and installed against the walls shown in the model views, to the dimensions and internal layouts set out below.

Overall carcass sizes, both 65 cm deep:

  • Ground floor — 200 cm wide × 250 cm high, in 2 equal bays of 100 cm.
  • First floor — 260 cm wide × 207 cm high, in 3 equal bays of 86.7 cm.

1 / 2

Every compartment sits behind doors. No niche or shelf is open to the room. Where the schematics read “open shelf” or “lower storage”, that means a compartment with no drawer front, still enclosed by the doors in front of it.

Ground-floor wardrobe, each bay from the top:

  1. 20 cm shelf compartment.
  2. 20 cm shelf compartment.
  3. 146 cm — full hanging rail in the left bay, adjustable shelving in the right bay.
  4. 18 cm drawer.
  5. 18 cm drawer.
  6. 28 cm lower storage compartment.

First-floor wardrobe, each bay from the top:

  1. 22 cm shelf compartment.
  2. 22 cm shelf compartment.
  3. 99 cm — short hanging rail in the left bay, adjustable shelving in the middle and right bays.
  4. 18 cm drawer.
  5. 18 cm drawer.
  6. 28 cm lower storage compartment.

The compartment heights add up to the full external height in both cases, 250 cm and 207 cm. Carcass, shelf, plinth and top-rail thicknesses should be absorbed within those figures rather than added to them.

1 / 2

Both wardrobes must be lit inside. This is a firm requirement, not an optional extra, and the wiring route has to be planned before the carcasses are built rather than added afterwards.

  • Warm white LED strip or bar in every bay of both wardrobes, positioned so the light source is not in direct view.
  • Door-activated switching, so a bay lights when its door is opened.
  • Drivers and cabling concealed within the carcass, with no visible trunking.
  • A concealed fused spur rather than a trailing plug lead. If there is no supply near either wall, that part of the work needs an electrician.

Materials and finish:

  • MFC for carcasses, shelves and drawer boxes, with matching ABS edging on visible edges.
  • MDF where it does the job better — plinths, back panels and scribe fillets.
  • Matt throughout. No gloss, and no woodgrain or patterned decor: plain solid colour.
  • Adjustable shelves on standard shelf pins, at a pitch that allows them to be moved later.

One solid colour per wardrobe. The values below are indicative for discussion only — a physical decor sample of each needs approving before anything is ordered.

  • Eucalyptus green #6e7f6d Ground-floor wardrobe.
  • Garnet red #733635 First-floor wardrobe.

Style is simple Scandinavian: quiet, solid and unfussy.

  • Shaker doors and drawer fronts throughout, on both wardrobes: a plain frame around a recessed flat centre panel. Flat slab fronts and fully flush trimless detailing are not wanted.
  • Slim plain handles, bar or tubular, in one finish across both wardrobes.
  • Hinged doors, two to a bay, with separate shorter doors over the upper compartments as drawn.
  • Soft-close hinges and drawer runners throughout.
  • Scribed to the walls, floor and ceiling. Both rooms should be assumed to be out of true.

A shaker front is a frame-and-panel door, so the fronts may have to be made in sprayed MDF rather than the MFC used for the carcasses. Either construction is acceptable, provided the door colour matches the carcass colour in the same matt finish.

On the first-floor wardrobe, both tall doors of the middle bay are to carry full-length mirrors. The mirror fills the shaker panel over the whole height of each door, rather than a small mirror set into a larger front. Safety-backed, and framed by the door itself rather than fixed on afterwards.

DIY fallback Not recommended

Two full-height fitted wardrobes in colour-matched MFC are past a sensible DIY scope. Panel cutting, edging and scribing to out-of-true walls decide whether the result looks fitted or homemade, and the carcasses are too tall and heavy to stand and secure without help. The preparation is worth doing though, and it shortens the build.

Fallback approach

  • Measure each wall at several heights and widths, and record the variation rather than a single figure.
  • Check the floor and ceiling for level across the full run and note the worst deviation.
  • Confirm skirting depth and anything in the footprint: sockets, switches, radiator pipes, vents.
  • Establish where the nearest power supply is on each wall, for the interior lighting.
  • Approve the colour samples and choose the handles.

Tools and supplies

  • Tape measure and a long spirit level or cross-line laser
  • Wall detector
  • Torch and a notepad for the survey

Stop and hand over if

  • The work moves from measuring to cutting, assembling or standing panels.
  • A wall, floor or ceiling is far enough out of true that the scribe has to be designed rather than trimmed.
  • The lighting needs a new fused spur or any alteration to fixed wiring.
  • A carcass would have to be lifted, stood or secured without at least two capable people.
