Install SimpliSafe bell box and video doorbell

Posted on 31 July 2026

The SimpliSafe bell box and Video Doorbell Pro are on site with an OhmKat plug-in power supply and wireless chime. Nothing is installed yet. Both devices go on the front: the bell box high, where it is seen from the driveway before anyone reaches the door; the doorbell beside the door, where it can see a visitor's face.

The bell box. The obvious spot above the front door is not available. The roof comes down to just above the door and window frames, leaving under 100 mm of bare wall below the gutter along the whole front.

1 / 2

The widest clear wall is the strip between the left-hand window and the door, which runs the full height. A bell box there sits at about 2.1 m — seen easily from the driveway, but within reach from the ground.

Settle before anything is drilled:

  • Whether 2.1 m on the front is good enough, or a higher spot on a side wall is worth having instead.
  • That the box and its plate fit that strip, which is narrow and already carries the house number plaque, and clear the gutter and downpipes.
  • How the box comes off its plate for battery changes. If it slides upward, the gutter directly above may leave too little room.
  • What is behind the white render, so the screws hold in something solid rather than in the render alone.
  • That nothing runs behind the screw positions. A utility cabinet sits at the foot of the same strip with two conduits dropping from it.

Pair the bell box and check that it sounds before it goes up. Once fixed, test that it sounds, sits firmly and reports back to the system.

The doorbell. A small dark fitting, about the size of a doorbell push, is already on the wall to the right of the door. Nobody has identified it. What it turns out to be decides whether the new doorbell replaces it, sits beside it, or goes on the other side.

1 / 2

Power does not come from the house wiring. The OhmKat supply plugs into an indoor socket and feeds the doorbell along a 5.5 m low-voltage cable; its wireless chime plugs into another socket indoors. Nothing connects to the mains or to an existing doorbell circuit.

Settle before anything is drilled:

  • Which side of the door gives the clearest view of someone at the door and of the approach from the driveway.
  • What the existing fitting is, before anything comes off the wall.
  • Whether the doorbell needs a wedge to face the right way, and whether one came in the box.
  • Which indoor socket feeds it, and whether the 5.5 m cable reaches from there without an extension.
  • How the cable gets through the wall without being trapped by the door, and how that hole is sealed.

Clip the cable or run it in trunking rather than leaving it loose. How the OhmKat transmitter joins the arrangement comes from its own instructions, not from guesswork on the day. Before the installer leaves: bell box firm and level, doorbell powered and answering a press, hole sealed, cable secure.

Materials

  • SimpliSafe bell box, already on site
  • SimpliSafe Video Doorbell Pro, already on site
  • OhmKat plug-in power supply with a 5.5 m cable, already on site
  • OhmKat wireless chime transmitter and receiver, already on site
  • Whatever mounting plates and fixings came in those boxes; no separate mounts were bought
  • Outdoor screws and wall plugs to suit whatever is behind the render
  • Exterior cable clips or narrow trunking
  • Outdoor sealant for the drilled holes
DIY fallback Limited

Pairing the devices, setting them up in the app and pairing the wireless chime are straightforward to do beforehand. Fixing them to the wall is not, because it means drilling a rendered wall from a ladder, close up under the gutter, and taking a cable through the wall.

Fallback approach

  • Pair the bell box with the system and confirm at ground level that it sounds.
  • Settle on the bell box position, given how little bare wall the roofline leaves.
  • Pick the indoor socket for the OhmKat supply and check that the 5.5 m cable reaches the intended doorbell position.
  • Add the doorbell in the app, connect it to the 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi, and check the view in daylight and after dark.
  • Pair the wireless chime and find a socket where it can be heard from the main rooms.
  • Clear the driveway so a ladder can be positioned safely.

Tools and supplies

  • Stable ladder suitable for the working height
  • Combi or SDS drill with masonry bits to suit the wall
  • Wall or service detector
  • Tape measure and spirit level
  • Outdoor fixings, cable clips and sealant
  • Eye and hearing protection and a second adult for ladder safety

Stop and hand over if

  • The bell box will not pair or sound correctly before it is fitted.
  • What is behind the render cannot be confirmed, so the screws might only hold in the render.
  • A scan suggests hidden wiring, pipework or metalwork where a screw would go.
  • The only workable spot for the bell box fouls the gutter or a downpipe, or leaves no room to take the box off for new batteries.
  • The 5.5 m cable will not reach, or there is no tidy, weatherproof way to bring it through the wall.
  • The ladder cannot stand safely.
