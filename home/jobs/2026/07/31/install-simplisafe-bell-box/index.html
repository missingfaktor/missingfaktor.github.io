Install SimpliSafe bell box

Posted on 31 July 2026

The SimpliSafe bell box has been bought but is not yet installed. It needs to go high on the front elevation, in the area above the front door and beneath the eaves.

The final position has to be:

  • Visible from the driveway and the street on approach.
  • High enough to be out of easy reach.
  • Still reachable by ladder for battery replacement and servicing.
  • Clear of gutters, downpipes, exterior lights, vents and opening windows.

Pair the bell box and check that it sounds at ground level before it goes up. Fixings must reach sound structural substrate rather than relying on render alone, so identify the wall construction before drilling and choose exterior-grade fixings to suit it.

Once it is mounted, a controlled system test confirms that the box sounds, sits firmly and reports correctly.

Materials

  • SimpliSafe bell box, already on site
  • Mounting plate and hardware supplied with the bell box
  • Exterior-grade screws and wall plugs suitable for the actual substrate
  • Exterior sealant for drilled penetrations
DIY fallback Limited

Pairing and sound-testing at ground level are straightforward to do beforehand. The permanent installation is not, because it means drilling an exterior wall from a ladder high on the front elevation.

Fallback approach

  • Pair the bell box with the system in advance.
  • Run a brief ground-level test to confirm that it sounds correctly.
  • Settle on the preferred area above the front door and beneath the eaves.
  • Clear the driveway so a ladder can be positioned safely.

Tools and supplies

  • Stable ladder suitable for the working height
  • Combi or SDS drill with substrate-appropriate masonry bits
  • Wall or service detector
  • Tape measure and spirit level
  • Exterior-grade fixings and sealant
  • Eye and hearing protection and a second adult for ladder safety

Stop and hand over if

  • The bell box will not pair or sound correctly before mounting.
  • The substrate beneath the render or exterior finish cannot be confirmed.
  • A scan suggests concealed wiring, pipework or metalwork at a fixing point.
  • The intended position is obstructed by a gutter, light, window or other fitting.
  • The ladder cannot stand safely, or the box would end up out of reach for future servicing.
