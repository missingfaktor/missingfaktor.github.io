Fix upstairs shower temperature control

Posted on 28 July 2026

The temperature handle on the upstairs shower will not turn past the middle of its travel towards hot, so the shower cannot be run any warmer than the 38C mark. The same handle is also loose on its spindle and rocks slightly when it is turned.

The valve is a concealed dual-control thermostatic mixer behind a single rectangular plate. The upper control selects off, rainhead or handset; the lower control sets temperature, marked H to one side and C to the other with 38C at the top. The illustration below shows that arrangement. It is not a photograph of the installed valve, and the make and model have not yet been identified.

Three causes need ruling out, in this order:

  1. The 38C limit stop. Many thermostatic valves hold at 38C behind a limit stop or override button that has to be pressed to go hotter. The first check is whether that stop is present, stuck, missing or set wrongly.
  2. The handle fixing. A slack grub screw or worn splines would explain the rocking, and can also stop the handle driving the spindle through its full range.
  3. Supply temperature and cartridge. If handle and stop are both sound, the question is whether hot water arrives at the valve at full temperature and whether the thermostatic cartridge is scaled or failed.

The remedy may be as small as re-seating the handle on the spindle, or may mean a new cartridge or a new valve. Replacing the valve body means opening the wall behind the plate and disturbing tiling, so a handle or cartridge repair is preferable if the valve can be identified.

Sourcing a part depends on that identification. Where to look:

  • A maker's mark on the plate, the handles, or the cartridge once the handle is off.
  • The paperwork from the bathroom installation.
  • Manufacturers' own spares lines and bathroom trade merchants.
  • Specialist shower-spares suppliers, several of which will match a cartridge from a photograph or the part in hand.

If the valve cannot be identified at all, the fallback is a replacement valve whose plate footprint covers the existing opening.

DIY fallback Limited

Checking the handle and the temperature limit stop is a reasonable first look, since it happens outside the valve and needs no pipework to be opened. Anything past that, including changing the cartridge or the valve body, is pressurised plumbing behind tiling and belongs to a plumber.

Fallback approach

  • Isolate the hot and cold supplies to the shower and confirm the outlet has stopped running before touching the handle.
  • Remove the temperature handle, usually a grub screw under a small cap, and photograph everything behind it, including any markings.
  • Check whether a limit stop ring or override catch is fitted, whether it is free to move, and whether the splines and grub screw are worn or slack.
  • Re-seat the handle square on the spindle with the 38C mark aligned, tighten the grub screw, restore the supplies and retest the range and the wobble.
  • If the range is still short with a sound handle and stop, stop there and pass on the photographs and any markings for the cartridge to be identified.

Tools and supplies

  • Small hex keys and a small flat screwdriver
  • Torch
  • Limescale remover suitable for chrome
  • Soft cloth to protect the plate
  • Camera or phone for reference photographs

Stop and hand over if

  • The supplies cannot be isolated locally.
  • The handle, cap or plate will not come away without force.
  • The work would mean removing the cartridge, the valve body or any tiling.
  • The temperature range is still limited after the handle and the limit stop have been ruled out.
